Fossil Layers and Mass Extinction Explorer

Explore fictional ecosystems, inspect rock layers and fossil diagrams, infer possible extinction boundaries, and test how environmental change can affect survival.

1. Choose an ecosystem

Visual student flow
  1. Choose an ecosystem from the visual preview and legend.
  2. Inspect at least five layers in the strata stack using diagrams, meters, and annotations.
  3. Tap or click between layers to mark the extinction boundary. Evidence may be clear or span several layers.
  4. Try again if needed. The answer is revealed after five attempts, with an explanation.
  5. Set up a simulation by choosing an environmental factor and adjusting rate and severity.
  6. Watch an automatic visualisation: changing landscape, indicator gauges, and a progress timeline.
  7. Compare runs side by side on the visual comparison board.

All ecosystems, species, and results are fictional and simplified. Fossils are clues; preservation varies and the record is incomplete. Results are temporary and clear when you reset or close the page.

2–3. Inspect strata and mark a boundary

Inspect each layer's annotated diagram and rock sample. Older layers are lower; younger layers are higher. Use the annotations to infer changes in fossil abundance and diversity rather than relying on exact counts.

6. Visual comparison board

Each time you run the simulation, a run card is saved here for side-by-side comparison until you reset or close the page. Compare settings, survival indicators, biodiversity mini graphs, and resulting fossil layer stacks.

Evidence note: A drop in fossil abundance or diversity can suggest extinction, but preservation varies and the fossil record is incomplete. Ask: what evidence in the diagrams led you to choose a boundary?
